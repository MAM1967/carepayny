On January 1, 2027, New York's minimum wage for home care aides goes up.

- New York City, Long Island and Westchester: **{{2027.min.nyc}} an hour** (up from {{2026.min.nyc}})
- The rest of the state: **{{2027.min.rest}} an hour** (up from {{2026.min.rest}})

That is {{2027.raise.nyc}} more an hour, or {{2027.raise.nyc.week40}} more for a 40-hour week at the minimum. The new rate is for hours worked on or after January 1. A pay period that started in December may show both rates.

## Who it covers

Agency aides, CDPAP caregivers paid through PPL, and most workers hired directly by a family. The general minimum wage for other jobs does not change on January 1.

## Wage parity

In New York City, Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester, wage parity for Medicaid-paid care also goes up, to a total of {{2027.wp.nyc.total}} an hour in New York City and {{2027.wp.liw.total}} on Long Island and in Westchester.

## A proposed raise in March

The governor has proposed raising rates again on March 1, 2027. It is not law. It needs the state legislature's approval. We will send an update if it passes.

## Checking January pay

The [pay calculator](/) uses the 2027 rates from January 1. Our page [New York home care minimum wage 2027](/pay/minimum-wage-2027/) has the details and the sources. The New York State Department of Labor takes wage claims, and [free legal help by region](/pay/legal-help/) lists groups that help with unpaid wages.
