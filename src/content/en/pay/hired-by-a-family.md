## What is the minimum wage for a home care worker hired by a family?

When a New York family hires a home care worker directly, the home care aide minimum wage applies. From January 1, 2027, it is {{2027.min.nyc}} an hour in New York City, Long Island and Westchester, and {{2027.min.rest}} an hour in the rest of the state. In 2026, it is {{2026.min.nyc}} and {{2026.min.rest}}. New York law defines a **home care aide** by the work: a worker whose main job is helping a person at home with daily activities, such as bathing, dressing or eating. The law does not depend on whether an agency or a family is the employer.

More on the rates: [New York home care minimum wage for 2027](/pay/minimum-wage-2027/).

## What does "casual basis" mean?

The home care aide minimum wage does not cover a person who works **on a casual basis**. That means work done only now and then, not as a regular job. The Department of Labor gives occasional babysitting as an example of this kind of work. For casual work, the general minimum wage applies instead. In 2027, the general minimum wage is {{2027.general.nyc}} an hour in New York City, Long Island and Westchester, and {{2027.general.rest}} an hour in the rest of the state.

The law also leaves out some relatives who are paid through a government program. CDPAP is different: CDPAP caregivers, including family members, are covered. More: [CDPAP pay rates](/cdpap/pay-rate/).

## When does overtime start for a worker hired by a family?

New York's law for **domestic workers** covers people a family hires to care for a sick, elderly or recovering person in the home. Overtime is paid at {{ot.times}}.

- A worker who does not live in the family's home gets overtime after {{ot.hours}} hours in a week.
- A worker who lives in the family's home gets overtime after {{ot.liveIn}} hours in a week.

Federal law does not require overtime for a live-in worker hired directly by a family. New York law still does. More: [Overtime for home care aides](/pay/overtime/).

## Does a worker hired by a family get a day off each week?

New York's domestic worker law gives one day of rest each week: 24 hours in a row. If the worker agrees to work on that day, the Department of Labor says those hours are paid at the overtime rate.

## How are 24-hour shifts paid for a family hire?

There are two rules to know:

- **The 13-hour rule.** For home care aides on 24-hour shifts, the Department of Labor allows pay for {{livein.hours}} hours only when the aide gets {{livein.sleep}} hours for sleep (at least 5 in a row) and {{livein.meals}} hours for meals. The state's highest court upheld it in 2019, in a case about agency aides. More: [24-hour shifts and the 13-hour rule](/pay/24-hour-shifts/).
- **The rule for live-in workers.** The state wage order says that a worker who lives in the employer's home is not working during normal sleeping hours just because they are on call. Time when the worker is free to leave is not work time either.

The [pay calculator](/) uses the 13-hour rule for all 24-hour shifts.

## Does wage parity apply when a family pays directly?

Usually not. Wage parity covers home care that Medicaid pays for. When a family pays for care with its own money, wage parity does not apply. More: [Wage parity in NYC, Long Island and Westchester](/pay/wage-parity/).

## Where do pay complaints go?

The New York State Department of Labor enforces the state's wage laws, including the domestic worker law. It takes wage claims from domestic workers.
