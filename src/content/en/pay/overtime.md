## When do home care aides get overtime in New York?

Home care aides who work for an agency, or for PPL in CDPAP, get overtime pay for hours over {{ot.hours}} in a week. Overtime is paid at {{ot.times}}. Since January 1, 2015, federal rules have required agencies to pay this overtime, even to aides who live in the client's home. A live-in worker hired directly by a family gets overtime after {{ot.liveIn}} hours in a week, under New York law.

| Who employs the worker | Overtime starts after | Overtime rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A home care agency | {{ot.hours}} hours a week | {{ot.multiplier}} times the regular rate |
| PPL (CDPAP) | {{ot.hours}} hours a week | {{ot.multiplier}} times the regular rate |
| A family, worker does not live in the home | {{ot.hours}} hours a week | {{ot.multiplier}} times the regular rate |
| A family, worker lives in the family's home | {{ot.liveIn}} hours a week | {{ot.multiplier}} times the regular rate |

## Do agencies have to pay overtime to live-in aides?

Yes. Federal law has an overtime exemption for some live-in workers. An **exemption** is a rule that says a law does not apply. Since January 1, 2015, only an individual, a family or a household can use it. A **third-party employer**, such as a home care agency, cannot. This is true even when the aide lives in the client's home, and even if the family is also an employer. So agencies pay overtime after {{ot.hours}} hours in a week.

## Does CDPAP pay overtime?

Yes. PPL is the employer that pays CDPAP caregivers, so the same {{ot.hours}}-hour rule applies. PPL also has its own rules on overtime, seventh-day overtime and holiday pay. More: [CDPAP overtime, holidays, sick leave and benefits](/cdpap/overtime-holidays-sick-leave/).

## When does overtime start for a worker hired by a family?

New York's law for **domestic workers** covers people a family hires to care for a sick, elderly or recovering person at home. Under that law:

- A worker who does not live in the family's home gets overtime after {{ot.hours}} hours in a week.
- A worker who lives in the family's home gets overtime after {{ot.liveIn}} hours in a week.

Federal law does not require overtime for a live-in worker hired directly by a family. New York law still does, after {{ot.liveIn}} hours. More: [Hired directly by a family](/pay/hired-by-a-family/).

## Does a domestic worker get a day off each week?

New York's domestic worker law gives one day of rest each week: 24 hours in a row. If the worker agrees to work on that day, the Department of Labor says those hours are paid at the overtime rate.

## Do 24-hour shifts count toward overtime?

Yes. The paid hours from 24-hour shifts count toward the weekly limit. For an agency aide, the {{livein.hours}} paid hours from each 24-hour shift are added to the other hours worked that week. Hours over {{ot.hours}} are overtime. More: [24-hour shifts and the 13-hour rule](/pay/24-hour-shifts/).

## Is there a way to check overtime for one week?

The [pay calculator](/) counts overtime hours for one week, based on who pays the worker and whether they live in the client's home. It saves nothing.
