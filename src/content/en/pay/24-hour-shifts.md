## How many hours is a 24-hour home care shift paid in New York?

A home care aide who works a 24-hour shift in New York can be paid for {{livein.hours}} hours, but only if the aide gets {{livein.sleep}} hours for sleep, with at least 5 hours of sleep in a row, and {{livein.meals}} hours for meals. If not, more hours must be paid, up to all 24. This is the **13-hour rule**. The New York State Department of Labor set it out in an opinion letter on March 11, 2010. The state's highest court, the Court of Appeals, upheld it on March 26, 2019.

## What does the 13-hour rule require?

The 13-hour rule has three parts. A 24-hour shift can be paid as {{livein.hours}} hours only when the aide:

- has {{livein.sleep}} hours for sleep,
- actually gets at least 5 of those hours as sleep without interruption, and
- has {{livein.meals}} hours for meals.

The sleep and meal hours are not paid when these conditions are met. All the other hours of the shift are paid.

## What happens if the aide's sleep is interrupted?

If the aide does not get at least 5 hours of sleep in a row, all {{livein.sleep}} sleep hours must be paid. For example, an aide who is woken up again and again to help the client during the night gets paid for the whole sleep period.

## What happens if the aide works through meal breaks?

Meal time can be left unpaid only when the aide actually gets the break. If the aide works through a meal break, that time must be paid. When both the sleep and meal conditions are missed, all 24 hours of the shift are paid.

## What did the Court of Appeals decide in 2019?

The case is called **Andryeyeva v. New York Health Care**. Home health aides who worked 24-hour shifts for agencies said they should be paid for all 24 hours. On March 26, 2019, the Court of Appeals accepted the Department of Labor's reading of the state's wage rules. So the 13-hour rule stands, with its conditions for sleep and meals.

## Who does the 13-hour rule cover?

The Department of Labor's letter and the court case were about home care aides who work 24-hour shifts. The court case was about aides employed by home care agencies.

- **CDPAP:** for live-in (per-diem) service codes, PPL pays {{livein.hours}} hours per day. More: [CDPAP pay rates](/cdpap/pay-rate/).
- **Hired by a family:** the state wage order has a separate rule for a worker who lives in the employer's home. Time asleep is not work time just because the worker is on call, and time when the worker is free to leave is not work time. More: [Hired directly by a family](/pay/hired-by-a-family/).

## Do 24-hour shift hours count toward overtime?

Yes. The paid hours from 24-hour shifts count toward the weekly hours for overtime. Hours over {{ot.hours}} in a week are paid at {{ot.times}}. More: [Overtime for home care aides](/pay/overtime/).

## Is there a way to check 24-hour shift pay?

The [pay calculator](/) asks how many 24-hour shifts were worked in a week, and on how many the sleep or meal breaks were missed. It then counts the paid hours. It saves nothing.
