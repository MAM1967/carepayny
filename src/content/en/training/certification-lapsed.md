A New York home health aide (HHA) certificate **lapses** after 24 months in a row without work as a home health aide for an approved agency. "Lapses" means it is no longer valid for work. A former aide whose certificate lapsed can take the skills test again instead of the whole 75-hour course. A personal care aide (PCA) certificate does not lapse.

## When does a home health aide certificate lapse?

It lapses when the aide has not worked as a home health aide for an **Article 36 or Article 40 agency** for 24 months in a row. These are home care agencies and hospices approved under New York's Public Health Law.

- The Department of Health says an aide stays certified while working for a licensed or certified agency.
- Work for a private family in New York does not count. Work in another state does not count either. In those cases, the certificate lapses two years after the last day of work for a New York home care agency.

## What are the options after a lapse?

A former home health aide with documented training and a passed skills test can take the **competency evaluation** again, instead of the full training course. The competency evaluation is the skills test. It has two parts:

- a written or spoken test, and
- showing skills while a nurse watches.

The passing score is 80%. A person who takes the written test again gets a different version of the test. If the person shows only some of the skills, the program gives training in the missing skills. Then it tests those skills again. A person who passes gets a new certificate.

## Who gives the competency evaluation?

Home health aide training programs approved by the Department of Health give it. An agency that runs an approved program can give the competency evaluation alone, without the course. A **nurse instructor** approved by the Department of Health must do the evaluation. That is a nurse the Department has approved to teach and test aides.

An approved home health aide training program may charge a trainee up to $100, for things the trainee keeps. Each program can explain what it charges, if anything, for the test alone.

## What does the Home Care Registry show?

The **Home Care Registry** is the Department of Health's list of people who finished an approved home health aide or personal care aide training program. By law, each listing includes:

- the person's name,
- each training program finished, who gave it and the date,
- work history in home care, with dates and employers,
- any final finding of abuse, neglect or taking a patient's property, and
- any Department decision on the background check.

The public can search the registry by name or by Home Care Registry number. Home addresses and dates of birth are not public. Some listings show a lapsed certificate or a background check that did not approve the person. The registry page tells searchers to look closely at anyone whose employment status is anything other than "YES."

**A worker's own record.** The law lets a home care worker see all the information in their own listing. The [registry search](https://apps.health.ny.gov/professionals/home_care/registry/home.action) is free. The law says no person or agency may be charged for any costs of the registry.

## How are mistakes in the registry fixed?

Training programs and agencies enter the information, not the Department of Health. The registry page says the Department does not guarantee information entered by others.

The rules say what happens when a worker finds a mistake:

- The worker gives the correct information to the program or agency that entered it, with proof if asked.
- For a name change, the worker gives proof, such as a marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order. The program or agency then changes the name in the registry.

**Lost certificate.** For aides trained on or after September 25, 2009, the training program can give a copy of the certificate. If the program cannot, or the training was more than six years ago, the Home Care Registry can print a **duplicate** (a copy with no signature) and send it to the aide. The Home Care Registry answers questions by email at hcreg@health.ny.gov.

## What do agencies check before hiring?

Before hiring, an agency must look up the aide in the Home Care Registry. A person who is not listed as a personal care aide, home health aide or advanced home health aide cannot be hired as one.

- **Home health aide:** the agency also checks that the certificate had not lapsed on the day of hire.
- **Personal care aide:** the certificate does not expire and does not lapse. The agency still checks that the training came from an approved program and keeps the records in the aide's file.
- **Advanced home health aide:** this certificate does not expire. But the aide must keep a valid home health aide certificate and finish 18 hours of training a year to keep advanced status.

A registry listing alone does not always show that a person can do the work. The agency also looks at the documents it has. If it cannot confirm the training, the person needs to finish an approved training program before being hired.

## Is a nurse aide (CNA) certificate different?

Yes. A **certified nurse aide** (CNA) is on a different list, the **Nurse Aide Registry**, not the Home Care Registry. A CNA certificate is renewed every two years. To renew, the CNA must have worked at least seven hours for pay as a nurse aide in the past 24 months. The facility or staffing agency that employs the CNA, or last employed them, pays the renewal fee.

The Department of Health's [Nurse Aide Registry page](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/consumer_information/nurse_aide_registry/using_the_nurse_aide_registry.htm) explains how to check a CNA's status.

## What is different in CDPAP?

In CDPAP, the **consumer** (the person who gets care) or their **designated representative** hires, trains, supervises and schedules the **personal assistant** (PA). The CDPAP rules do not require a home health aide or personal care aide certificate. More: [Signing up as a CDPAP PA](/cdpap/signing-up/).
