New York has three main aide certificates. A **home health aide** (HHA) and a **personal care aide** (PCA) work in home care, and both go on the Home Care Registry. A **certified nurse aide** (CNA) trains to work in nursing homes and goes on a different list, the Nurse Aide Registry. CDPAP needs none of the three.

## How do the three compare?

| | Home health aide (HHA) | Personal care aide (PCA) | Certified nurse aide (CNA) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main workplace | Home care agencies, hospices | Home care agencies | Nursing homes; other health care facilities licensed under Article 28 of the Public Health Law |
| Training | At least 75 hours, including 16 hands-on hours | At least 40 hours | At least 100 hours, including 30 hands-on hours in a nursing home (Education Department adult programs: at least 120) |
| Who approves programs | Department of Health or Education Department | Department of Health or Education Department | Department of Health or Education Department |
| Test | Written or spoken test, plus skills shown to a nurse | Written or spoken test, plus skills shown to a nurse | State exam: a clinical skills test and a written (or oral) test |
| List the person goes on | Home Care Registry | Home Care Registry | Nursing Home Nurse Aide Registry |
| Does it expire? | Yes, after 24 months in a row without HHA work for an agency | No | Renewed every 2 years |

## What does each aide do?

- **Home health aide services** are simple health care tasks, personal hygiene and housework needed for the patient's health, under a nurse's supervision.
- **Personal care services** are help with hygiene, dressing, eating and household tasks, also supervised by a nurse. Personal care aides may do fewer health-related tasks than home health aides.
- A **certified nurse aide** works with **residents** (people who live in a nursing home). Nursing homes must check the Nurse Aide Registry, and so must other health care facilities licensed under **Article 28** of the Public Health Law. Article 28 is the part of state law for hospitals and nursing homes.

## How long is each training?

A home health aide course is at least 75 hours, and a personal care aide course is at least 40 hours. More: [Training and requirements](/training/).

A CNA course is at least 100 hours under Department of Health rules: at least 70 hours in class and lab, and 30 hours of **supervised clinical training**. That means caring for nursing home residents while an instructor watches. The Education Department's guidelines for adult programs list at least 120 hours: 90 in class and 30 clinical.

## Who approves the programs, and what do they cost?

For all three, a program is approved either by the New York State Department of Health or by the New York State Education Department.

- **HHA:** a Department of Health program may charge up to $100, for things the trainee keeps. A program that charges more is approved by the Education Department.
- **PCA:** a Department of Health program may not charge any fee or tuition.
- **CNA:** Department of Health programs are free to the trainee, including the first try at the exam. A nursing home may not charge training or testing fees to its workers or to people it has offered a job. Programs that charge tuition are approved by the Education Department.

More: [Approved HHA and PCA programs](/training/programs/) · [Free training](/training/free-training/)

## What is the CNA exam?

The CNA exam is the state's **nurse aide competency exam**. It has two parts: a clinical skills test and a written (or oral) test. The trainee must pass both. Under Department of Health rules, a trainee gets three tries at each part. The company Prometric runs the exam for the Department of Health.

## Which registry does each aide go on?

- **Home Care Registry:** the Department of Health's list of people who finished an approved HHA or PCA program. Home care agencies and hospices check it before an aide starts.
- **Nursing Home Nurse Aide Registry:** the list of people New York has found able to work as nurse aides. A person who passes both parts of the CNA exam is added. It also lists some findings of abuse or neglect of residents. Prometric manages it for the Department of Health.

A CNA renews every 2 years. To renew, the aide must have worked at least 7 paid hours as a nurse aide in the past 24 months.

## Can a CNA work as a home health aide?

Not with the CNA certificate alone. Home health aide work for a home care agency takes a home health aide certificate on the Home Care Registry. But a CNA on the New York Nurse Aide Registry does not repeat what the nurse aide course already taught.

- An HHA training program adds the skills the nurse aide course did not cover. Examples are helping with medicines, handling a patient's money and handling emergencies in a home.
- The program first checks that the CNA can do those skills. Then it gives the HHA certificate.
- Some people can take only the HHA skills test, without the course. Examples are a nursing assistant with one year of full-time work in a general hospital in the past five years, and a person with HHA or nurse aide training from another state.

## Can an HHA or PCA become a CNA?

An HHA or PCA certificate does not count as a CNA certificate. Under Department of Health guidance, a nurse aide program may let a person who finished an HHA or PCA course skip some basic lessons. The program checks the person's skills first. All clinical skills must still be shown.

**PCA to HHA:** a person with a PCA certificate can take a 35-hour upgrade course to become an HHA. More: [Training and requirements](/training/).

## Do the pay rules differ?

Yes, in which law covers the job. New York's **home care aide minimum wage** covers home health aides, personal care aides, CDPAP personal assistants and any other worker whose main job includes help at home with daily activities. From January 1, 2027, it is {{2027.min.nyc}} an hour in New York City, Long Island and Westchester, and {{2027.min.rest}} an hour in the rest of the state.

A CNA who works in a nursing home or a hospital does not give care in a person's home. That job falls under the state's general minimum wage law, which covers every employer. A CNA who works as a home care aide is covered by the home care aide minimum. More: [New York home care minimum wage for 2027](/pay/minimum-wage-2027/).

## Does CDPAP need any of these certificates?

No. In CDPAP, the rules do not require an HHA, PCA or CNA certificate. The **consumer** (the person who gets care) or their representative trains the **personal assistant**. More: [What is different in CDPAP](/training/) · [Signing up as a CDPAP PA](/cdpap/signing-up/).
