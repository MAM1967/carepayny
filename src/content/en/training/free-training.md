In New York, a personal care aide (PCA) training program approved by the Department of Health may not charge any fee or tuition. A home health aide (HHA) program approved by the Department of Health may charge up to $100, only for things the trainee keeps. Programs approved by the New York State Education Department, such as licensed private career schools, are allowed to charge tuition.

## Which programs can charge, and how much?

It depends on which state agency approved the program. A program is approved by the Department of Health or by the Education Department, never both.

| Approved by | Personal care aide (PCA) | Home health aide (HHA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Department of Health | No fee or tuition | No tuition. A fee of up to $100 for things the trainee keeps |
| Education Department | Tuition allowed | Tuition allowed |

The $100 HHA fee covers things like books, supplies and equipment. The trainee keeps them when they finish or leave the program. The program must be able to show what those things really cost. A home health aide program that plans to charge more than $100 must get approval from the Education Department instead.

The programs approved by the Department of Health are run by home care agencies and hospices. Our [list of approved places](/training/programs/) comes from the Department of Health's list.

## Why do some agencies train people for free?

Some home care agencies train people for free and plan to hire them after. Some training comes with a **work agreement**. That is a promise to work for the agency for a set time after training. Whether a program comes with a work agreement, and what it says, is something each program can explain.

## Is there a law about paying back training costs?

Yes. From December 19, 2026, a state law called the **Trapped at Work Act** bans employers from making workers or job applicants sign an **employment promissory note** as a condition of the job. That is an agreement to pay the employer money if the job ends before a set date.

The law allows some agreements to pay back the cost of tuition, fees and required materials for a **transferable credential**. That is a credential that can be used at other jobs, not only with this employer. These agreements have to meet rules, including:

- the agreement is a written contract, separate from the job contract,
- the worker is not required to get the training credential as a condition of the job,
- the amount to pay back is set before the worker agrees, and is not more than the employer's cost,
- the amount goes down over time while the worker stays,
- the worker does not pay back if the employer fires them, unless it is for misconduct.

The law applies to agreements made, or changed, on or after December 19, 2026. The New York State Department of Labor enforces it and can fine employers.

## What is a licensed private career school?

A **private career school** (also called a proprietary school) trains people for jobs but does not give college degrees. In New York, the Education Department's **Bureau of Proprietary School Supervision** (BPSS) licenses these schools. Licensed schools can teach PCA and HHA courses and charge tuition.

Students at licensed schools get state protections:

- state-approved courses and licensed teachers,
- refund rules set by state law,
- a state **Tuition Reimbursement Account**, which can pay back tuition in some cases,
- a way to complain to the state.

Students at unlicensed schools do not get these protections. Since August 18, 2025, a student starting a PCA or HHA course at a licensed school signs a PCA/HHA addendum (an extra page to the enrollment agreement) before class starts.

**Checking a school.** BPSS can say whether a school is licensed. Its phone numbers are (212) 643-4760 and (518) 474-3969, and its email is BPSS@nysed.gov. Complaints about a school go to the Education Department or the Department of State.

## Do community colleges offer home care aide training?

Yes, some do. The Education Department keeps a list of approved home health aide programs by region. Most are at community colleges, CUNY colleges and Educational Opportunity Centers. One is taught in Spanish.

Two examples:

- Kingsborough Community College (CUNY) has an HHA course. Lectures are online and the lab and clinical practice are in person.
- SUNY Schenectady County Community College has a 40-hour PCA course.

Both list tuition on their websites, with the current cost and dates.

## Can union members get free training?

The **1199SEIU Training and Employment Funds** were set up by the union 1199SEIU and employers. They offer training for 1199SEIU members, including home care workers, plus English and citizenship classes. Their Home Care Industry Education Fund offers college programs "at little or no cost." Who qualifies is something the funds explain. Their phone number is (844) TEF-1199.

## Can a Career Center help pay for training?

Sometimes. New York State **Career Centers** are local job centers. Many offer free help with finding a job, in person or online. They also run training help under the **Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act** (WIOA), a federal job training law.

- An **Individual Training Account** is WIOA money that pays a training provider for a person's course.
- The provider must be on the state's **Eligible Training Provider List**. Anyone can search this list by course or provider.
- Adults 18 and older and people who lost their jobs may qualify. Funding depends on many things, including a review of the person's job needs.
- Being on the list does not promise that money is available.

A Career Center decides whether a person qualifies. The Department of Labor's Career Center page lists centers by county.

## What can programs explain before someone signs up?

Programs can explain:

- whether the state approval is from the Department of Health or the Education Department,
- whether the course is HHA, PCA or the PCA-to-HHA upgrade,
- the total cost, what any fee covers and what the trainee keeps,
- whether a work agreement comes with the training, for how long and what happens if the job ends early,
- the refund rules, for programs that charge tuition,
- the language of the class,
- the schedule: days, nights or weekends, and where hands-on practice happens,
- which parts are online and which are in person. Our [program list](/training/programs/) marks programs approved as "Part online,"
- when the program adds a graduate to the Home Care Registry.
