To work as an aide for a home care agency in New York, a person needs a certificate from a state-approved training program. There are two certificates. A **home health aide** (HHA) needs at least 75 hours of training. A **personal care aide** (PCA) needs at least 40 hours. CDPAP is different: its rules do not require either certificate.

More: [Home health aide vs PCA vs CNA](/training/hha-pca-cna/).

## What are the steps?

| Step | What it involves |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Training | Home health aide: at least 75 hours. Personal care aide: at least 40 hours. |
| 2. Skills test | A written or spoken test, and showing skills to a nurse |
| 3. Certificate | The training program gives the certificate and adds the person to the Home Care Registry |
| 4. Checks at hiring | The agency checks the registry, runs a background check and checks ID and permission to work |
| 5. Health check | Before the first patient |
| 6. Every year | Training on the job, a health check and a job review |

## What is the difference between the two trainings?

**Home health aide.** The training is at least 75 hours: 59 hours in class and 16 hours of **supervised practical training**. That means hands-on practice while a nurse instructor watches. The training and the skills test must be finished within 60 calendar days of the first day.

**Personal care aide.** The training is at least 40 hours. The training and the skills test must be finished within 90 calendar days of the first day.

Tests and breaks do not count toward the training hours.

**From personal care aide to home health aide.** A person with a personal care aide certificate can take a 35-hour upgrade course to become a home health aide: 19 hours in class and 16 hours of supervised practical training. The Department of Health says a trainee cannot go straight from a personal care aide course into the upgrade course. Past work as a personal care aide does not replace the 16 practical hours.

## What is the skills test?

The skills test is called a **competency evaluation**. It has two parts:

- a written or spoken test, and
- showing skills while a nurse watches.

The passing score is 80%.

## Who gives the training, and what does it cost?

- Home care agencies and hospices licensed in New York can run training programs approved by the Department of Health.
- Schools can run training programs approved by the New York State Education Department.
- A home health aide program approved by the Department of Health may charge a trainee up to $100, for things the trainee keeps. A program that charges more must be approved by the Education Department instead.
- A personal care aide program approved by the Department of Health may not charge any fee or tuition.

More: [Approved training programs, by borough and county](/training/programs/) (from the Department of Health's list) · [Free vs paid training](/training/free-training/).

**Training in other languages.** Approved programs can teach in a language other than English. The nurse instructor must speak both English and that language well. Each class is taught in one language, without an interpreter.

## What is the Home Care Registry?

The **Home Care Registry** is the Department of Health's list of people who finished an approved home health aide or personal care aide training program. The training program adds each person who passes and makes the certificate through the registry.

- A person who finished training cannot give care until the program has added them to the registry.
- An agency must look up a new aide in the registry before the aide starts working for it.
- Within 10 business days after hiring, the agency adds the new job to the registry.

## What is the background check?

Home care agencies must run a **criminal history record check** for workers who give care to patients. That is a check of a person's criminal records. The agency asks the Department of Health to run it. The person's fingerprints are checked against New York State and FBI records.

The Department of Health looks at any record it finds and decides if the person can work in home care. The New York State Comptroller looked at checks from April 2014 to December 2016. About 83% of the checks found no criminal record. In about 3.5% of the checks, the person was found not able to work in home care.

More: [Home care background checks: what to expect](/training/background-check/).

**Identity and permission to work.** The agency also checks each new worker's ID. Like every employer in the United States, it checks permission to work with Form I-9.

## What is in the health check?

Before an aide cares for patients, the agency checks the aide's health. The rules include:

- a health check to make sure the aide's health is not a risk to patients,
- a test for tuberculosis (TB): a skin test or a blood test,
- proof of protection against measles (for people born on or after January 1, 1957) and rubella, through vaccine records or a blood test,
- during flu season, a flu vaccine or wearing a mask.

After that, there is a health check and a TB check every year.

## What happens every year on the job?

- **In-service training** is training given while a person works. Home health aides must have 12 hours a year. Personal care aides must have 6 hours a year.
- The agency reviews each aide's work every year. The review includes at least one visit to a patient's home to watch the aide work, if that applies.

## Does a home health aide certificate expire?

A home health aide is no longer qualified after 24 months in a row without working as a home health aide for a licensed or certified home care agency. To work as a home health aide again, the person can take the skills test again instead of the whole course. They then get a new certificate.

A personal care aide certificate does not expire.

More: [Lapsed home health aide certificate](/training/certification-lapsed/).

## What is different in CDPAP?

In CDPAP, the rules do not require a home health aide or personal care aide certificate. The **consumer** (the person who gets care) or their **designated representative** trains, supervises and schedules the **personal assistant** (PA).

- A spouse, a person legally responsible for the consumer or the consumer's designated representative cannot be the consumer's PA.
- PAs still have a health check before they start, and PPL has its own required training. More: [Signing up as a CDPAP PA](/cdpap/signing-up/).
