New York home care agencies must run a fingerprint background check on new aides. The Department of Health looks at any record and decides if the person can work in home care. A criminal record does not automatically stop someone from working: the law asks the Department to weigh each case, and most checks find no record at all.

## What is the criminal history record check?

The **criminal history record check** (CHRC) is a check of a person's criminal records, using their **fingerprints**. The home care agency asks the New York State Department of Health to run it. The fingerprints are checked against New York State records and FBI records.

The rules apply to certified home health agencies, licensed home care services agencies, long term home health care programs and hospices. They also apply to nursing homes and adult homes. The rules are in 10 NYCRR Part 402.

## What happens before the fingerprints?

- The agency asks the person to sign a statement. The statement lists any criminal conviction, in New York or anywhere else, and any past finding of patient abuse.
- The agency tells the person in writing that a check is needed. It also explains the right to see and correct their criminal record.
- The person signs a Department of Health consent form.
- For the fingerprints, the person shows two government IDs. One must have their signature and at least one must have a photo.

A person can withdraw their job application at any time before a job is offered or turned down. The rules say this happens "without prejudice," which means it does not count against them.

## Who pays for the background check?

The agency pays. By law, the agency may not ask the worker to pay the check fees or the agency's costs, directly or indirectly. This applies to new, temporary and current workers.

## Can a person work while the check is pending?

Yes, in many cases. A home care agency may give **temporary approval** while it waits for the result. During that time, the agency must watch and check the new aide's work every week. In the first week, someone from the agency visits the patient's home in person. After that, a home visit happens at least every other week. In the weeks between, the check can be a home visit or a phone call to the patient or the patient's representative.

There are exceptions. If the person has a **pending charge** (a criminal case that is not finished) for a felony, the Department puts its decision on hold until the case ends. For a pending misdemeanor charge, it may do the same. While a decision is on hold, the person may not give direct care.

## What can the Department of Health decide?

| Result | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| No criminal record | The Department does not deny. The agency makes its own hiring choice. |
| A record, no denial | The Department does not propose a denial. The agency decides, following Correction Law Article 23-A. |
| On hold | A charge is still open. The decision waits until it ends. |
| Proposed denial | The Department plans to deny. The person can explain in writing first. |
| Final denial | The Department tells the agency not to hire the person, or to end a temporary job. |

For some serious crimes, the Department must propose a denial unless it decides the person poses no risk. Examples are felony sex offenses at any time and violent felonies in the last 10 years. For other crimes, the Department may propose a denial, but only in line with **Article 23-A** of the Correction Law. Article 23-A is the New York law that protects people with past convictions from unfair job denials.

## What information does the check look at?

Executive Law § 845-b lists what the check covers: pending criminal charges and convictions that were not vacated or reversed. It also includes certificates of relief and good conduct on file with the state, and FBI records.

The New York State Department of Labor describes the **RAP sheet** (Record of Arrest and Prosecution). It is a person's state criminal record. People can ask for a copy to see what employers may see and to find mistakes. A fee waiver is available.

## What happens after a proposed denial?

The Department sends a written summary of the criminal record to the agency and to the person. The person also gets a copy of Article 23-A and information on how to correct wrong records.

The person then has **30 calendar days** from the mailing date to send the Department a written explanation before it makes a final decision. The agency may not let the person give direct care until the Department makes a final decision.

## What factors does the law weigh?

Correction Law § 753 lists eight factors:

1. New York's policy of encouraging work for people with past convictions
2. The duties of the job
3. How the offense relates to doing those duties
4. How much time has passed since the offense
5. The person's age at the time
6. How serious the offense was
7. Proof of **rehabilitation** (positive change since the offense) and good conduct
8. Protecting people's safety and property

The Department of Labor says a violation of Article 23-A by an employer counts as illegal discrimination. The New York State Division of Human Rights takes discrimination complaints. For something that happened on or after February 15, 2024, a complaint can be filed up to three years after it happened.

## What are certificates of relief and good conduct?

These certificates remove some legal bars that a conviction creates. Under § 753, the Department and employers must consider them. They create a **presumption of rehabilitation**. That means the law starts from the view that the person has changed, for the crimes the certificate covers.

| Certificate | Who it is for | Who gives it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Certificate of Relief from Disabilities** | Any number of misdemeanors and no more than one felony | The court that handled the case, or DOCCS (the state prison and parole agency) |
| **Certificate of Good Conduct** | People with more than one felony too | DOCCS, after a waiting period: 1 year (misdemeanors only), 3 years (C, D or E felony) or 5 years (A or B felony) |

DOCCS says a certificate does not promise a job. It restores the right to apply and be considered. DOCCS explains the forms and rules on its website.

## How often are people denied?

The New York State Comptroller looked at these checks for health care workers, including home care, from April 2014 to December 2016. About 83% of the checks found no criminal record. In about 3.5% of the checks, the person was found not able to work in these jobs. So most people who did have a record were not denied.

## Is there a background check in CDPAP?

The CDPAP rules (18 NYCRR § 505.28) do not list a criminal history record check for personal assistants. They do require a health check before work starts. PPL explains its own hiring steps for PAs. More: [Signing up as a CDPAP PA](/cdpap/signing-up/).

## Where can people get legal help?

- The **Legal Action Center** is a New York City organization. It gives legal services on job discrimination because of a criminal record, and on learning what is on a record.
- Some legal aid offices help people get and correct their RAP sheet at no cost, if they qualify by income.
