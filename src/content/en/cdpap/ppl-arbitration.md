In November 2026, PPL started asking CDPAP personal assistants (PAs) to accept a **Dispute Resolution Agreement**. It is an **arbitration agreement**. A PA who accepts it agrees to settle future wage and work disputes with PPL in private, one-on-one arbitration, not in court or in a class action. A PA has 30 days to opt out. There is no single deadline for everyone. Each PA's 30 days start on the day that PA selects "Acknowledge."

## What is PPL's Dispute Resolution Agreement?

It is a new agreement PPL is asking every PA to accept. Under it, future wage and work claims against PPL go to private arbitration, one worker at a time, instead of to court or a class action.

- **Arbitration** means a private person, called an arbitrator, decides the dispute instead of a judge or jury.
- A **class action** is a court case where a few workers sue for a large group. The $162 million [PPL settlement](/cdpap/ppl-settlement/) came from a class action.

## How does the Time4Care pop-up work?

- PPL emailed PAs about the agreement around November 11, 2026.
- A pop-up shows the agreement when a PA logs in to submit time.
- During the first week, the pop-up can be closed with the "X" at the top. After that, PPL's email says the PA must select "Acknowledge" to keep going.
- Acknowledging means the PA received the agreement. NY Health Access says it is not the same as agreeing to arbitration.
- Acknowledging starts the 30-day opt-out window.

## What happens if a PA does nothing?

If 30 days pass after acknowledging and the PA has not opted out, the PA is bound by the agreement. NY Health Access says that means future wage disputes with PPL go to arbitration. A PA who opts out keeps the right to take future wage claims to court.

## How does opting out work?

PPL has not given PAs an opt-out form. NY Health Access reports these steps:

1. **Write a short notice** saying the PA declines the Dispute Resolution Agreement and declines arbitration for any disputes.
2. **Include:** full name, address, PPL ID number, date of birth and the last 4 digits of the Social Security number.
3. **Send it** by email to **nycdpap_dra@pplfirst.com**, or by mail to PPL, 17 Plaza Drive, Suite 300, Latham, NY 12110.
4. **Send it within 30 days** of acknowledging. The date it is sent is what counts.
5. **Keep proof.** For email, a delivery receipt. For mail, certified mail with a return receipt.

The opt-out address has "_dra" in it. It is not PPL's customer service address (nycdpap@pplfirst.com). The agreement shown in the pop-up also explains how to opt out.

## What are the two choices?

| | Accept (do nothing) | Opt out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A future wage claim against PPL | Private arbitration, one worker at a time | Court, alone or as part of a class action |
| What it takes | Nothing after acknowledging | An email or letter within 30 days |

## Does the agreement affect the $162 million settlement?

The settlement covers PAs in NYC, Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester for work from March 1, 2025 to April 30, 2026. The agreement is about future disputes.

A federal court blocked PPL from sending the agreement to settlement class members until the settlement had final approval. The court said it could confuse workers and lead some to drop out of the settlement by mistake. Final approval came on November 10, 2026.

The sources we checked do not say how the agreement affects other wage lawsuits against PPL that are still in court.
