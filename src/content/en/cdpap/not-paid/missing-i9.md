## What you see

PPL@Home shows the timesheet as "Pended," with the reason **Provider is not Paperwork Complete.** The I-9 is one of the forms that can cause this.

## What the I-9 is

Form I-9 is a U.S. government form. Every employer in the U.S. must have one for each worker. It shows two things:

- **Who you are** (identity)
- **That you are allowed to work in the U.S.** (work authorization)

PPL is the PA's employer on paper, so PPL must have a completed I-9 with documents before it can pay. This is true even for a PA who cares for a family member, and even for a PA who had an I-9 with a CDPAP company before PPL.

## What documents the I-9 accepts

The U.S. government (USCIS) sets the list. A worker shows either:

- **One document from List A.** It proves both identity and work permission. Examples: a U.S. passport, or a Permanent Resident Card ("green card").

**or**

- **One document from List B plus one from List C.**
  - List B proves identity. Example: a driver's license or state ID card.
  - List C proves work permission. Examples: a Social Security card without work limits, or a U.S. birth certificate.

The full list is on the USCIS website. In most cases, documents must be originals (not copies) and not expired.

## Where the records are

PPL@Home lists the I-9 and shows if it is complete.

## Who handles each part

- **The PA:** fills out their part of the I-9 and shows the documents
- **PPL:** checks the documents and marks the I-9 complete. PPL's phone line is 1-833-247-5346.
