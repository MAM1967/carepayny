## Two numbers: the law and PPL's rate

- The **state minimum** is the lowest pay the law allows for home care aides. CDPAP caregivers are covered by it. The state sets it.
- **PPL's base pay** is the rate PPL says it pays CDPAP caregivers. In New York City, Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester, it is higher than the state minimum.

PPL's rate depends on where the **consumer** lives, not where the PA lives. PPL says its rates are set, not negotiated. The PA's offer letter lists their base rate.

## 2027 rates (starting January 1, 2027)

The state minimum for home care aides goes up on January 1, 2027.

| Where the consumer lives | State minimum (the law) | PPL's base pay for CDPAP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New York City | {{2027.min.nyc}} an hour | PPL has not posted 2027 rates yet |
| Nassau, Suffolk or Westchester | {{2027.min.liw}} an hour | PPL has not posted 2027 rates yet |
| Any other county | {{2027.min.rest}} an hour | PPL has not posted 2027 rates yet |

- PPL's 2026 rates in New York City ({{2026.ppl.nyc}}) and Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester ({{2026.ppl.liw}}) are still above the 2027 state minimum.
- In other counties, PPL's 2026 rate ({{2026.ppl.rest}}) is below the 2027 state minimum. From January 1, 2027, the law requires at least {{2027.min.rest}}.
- We will add PPL's 2027 rates here when PPL posts them.

The general minimum wage for most other jobs does not change on January 1, 2027. It stays {{2027.general.nyc}} in New York City, Long Island and Westchester, and {{2027.general.rest}} in the rest of the state.

The governor has proposed another raise on March 1, 2027. It needs the state legislature's approval. If it passes, we will update this page.

## Wage parity (NYC, Long Island and Westchester)

In New York City, Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester, a state law called the **Wage Parity Law** adds to the minimum. It covers Medicaid-paid home care, including CDPAP. The extra amount stays the same in 2027. The total goes up because the minimum goes up.

| Area | Extra wage parity amount | Total per hour, 2026 | Total per hour, 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New York City | {{2027.wp.nyc.benefit}} | {{2026.wp.nyc.total}} | {{2027.wp.nyc.total}} |
| Nassau, Suffolk or Westchester | {{2027.wp.liw.benefit}} | {{2026.wp.liw.total}} | {{2027.wp.liw.total}} |

The employer can pay the extra amount as cash, as benefits, or as a mix of both. PPL says its rates take wage parity into account. PPL pays part of it as cash (its base pay is above the minimum) and part as benefits. Examples from PPL's policies:

- **Paid sick leave:** part of each hour's wage parity amount goes toward paid sick leave.
- **Retirement:** PAs in these areas who are not in a PPL health plan get wage parity money put into a **401(a)** retirement account.

More on benefits: [Overtime, holidays, sick leave and benefits](/cdpap/overtime-holidays-sick-leave/).

Wage parity does not apply in the rest of the state.

## Overtime

Hours over {{ot.hours}} in a week are paid at {{ot.times}}. PPL has extra rules on overtime and holiday pay. They are on [Overtime, holidays, sick leave and benefits](/cdpap/overtime-holidays-sick-leave/).

## Live-in days

For live-in (per-diem) service codes, PPL pays a flat {{livein.hours}} hours per day. Only one PA per day gets that payment.
